Module 5 Discussion — Build a Reliable Sensor Message

Use binary representation, parity, exact storage, and information content.

How this discussion works

Solve, present, then respond

Work the problem below before reading classmates’ solutions. In your initial post, show enough mathematical work that another student can follow your method, then state the result in the language of the situation. Post your solution in the class discussion board; this page itself does not submit responses.

A clear table, calculation, labeled graph, or photo of neatly organized handwritten work is fine when appropriate. The goal is mathematical communication, not extra prose.

Context

The problem

A remote environmental sensor sends one short message each minute. It has 8 status states, numbered 0 through 7. Choose any one state number. The status is transmitted as a 3-bit binary code followed by an even parity bit. The sensor also records temperature to the nearest tenth of a degree; choose one temperature from 18.7°C, 21.4°C, or 23.9°C. A rare emergency flag occurs with probability 1/32.
Initial post

What to include

  1. Convert your chosen status number to a 3-bit binary code.
  2. Append the correct even parity bit and write the complete 4-bit block.
  3. Store your chosen temperature as an integer number of tenths of a degree. Explain why this avoids repeatedly approximating a decimal tenth in binary.
  4. Compute the information content, in bits, of the emergency event with probability 1/32.
  5. Suppose the status log for eight minutes is NNNNNNAA. Write its run-length encoding and explain what information is being compressed.
Replies

Engage with classmates’ mathematics

Check a classmate’s status code and parity bit. If they chose a different state or temperature, compare the representations and identify which parts of the message length stay fixed and which depend on the data.

A useful reply checks a calculation, compares methods or assumptions, or asks a mathematically specific question. Avoid replies that only say that you agree.
