Module 5 Discussion — When Measurement Changes Behavior

Use two recursive sequences to investigate what changes when a school receives progress reports one week late.

How this discussion works

Context

When the information is one week old

A tutoring program aims to help a class reach a mastery score of 80. Suppose its simplified update rule is

\[\text{next score}=\text{current score}+0.5(80-\text{measured score}).\]

Begin at score \(x_0=56\). With immediate information, the measured score is the current score. With one-week-old information, the measured score is the previous week's score. For the delayed model, assume \(x_{-1}=x_0=56\).

These numbers form a model for exploring feedback. Real student learning is more complicated, and no teaching program can guarantee that an update produces an exact score.

Your initial post

Make a claim using a sequence

  1. Complete the two numerical sequences through week 4. Show at least one immediate-feedback calculation and one delayed-feedback calculation.
  2. Describe what would happen if you plotted each sequence against week number. Which one reaches the target without crossing it? Which one overshoots?
  3. Explain why the delayed sequence overshoots, referring to the particular earlier score used in one of the updates.
  4. Connect the model to statistics. How could an unusually easy quiz lead to a misleading report about mastery? How might averaging several assessments help, and what might be the cost of waiting?
  5. Give an example of a different feedback system in school, nature, or society where delayed information might cause a correction to arrive too late.
