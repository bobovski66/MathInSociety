Making adjustments

What helps us decide whether to make a change—or give it more time?

FeedbackDelayEveryday choices

Start with something familiar

You turn up a shower because the water feels cold. Nothing seems to happen, so you turn it up again. A few seconds later, the water is too hot.

Each time, you used how the water felt to decide what to do next. That is feedback. But the water took time to respond.

Your post

Choose something people adjust—such as room temperature, a recipe, a practice routine, or the amount of water a plant gets. Your example can be real or made up.

Use your own example and answer all the numbered questions. Post your response in Canvas.

  1. Describe what someone is trying to get right. What tells them whether their change is working?
  2. What could happen if they make a change that is too big?
  3. What could happen if it takes a while to see the result?
  4. Would everyone agree on what counts as “better”? Explain your thinking.

Reply to a classmate

Read a classmate’s example. Ask a question, suggest a small change, or connect their example to something you have noticed. Give them something to respond to.

You might start with:

  • “What would happen if you waited longer before changing it again?”
  • “I think that could work because… Have you also thought about ___?”

Explain a little of your thinking so your classmate can build on it.
