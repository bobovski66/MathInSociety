Module 4 Discussion — Individual Measurements and Population Claims

Separate a z-score question from a confidence-interval question.

How this discussion works

Solve, present, then respond

Work the problem below before reading classmates’ solutions. In your initial post, show enough mathematical work that another student can follow your method, then state the result in the language of the situation. Post your solution in the class discussion board; this page itself does not submit responses.

A clear table, calculation, labeled graph, or photo of neatly organized handwritten work is fine when appropriate. The goal is mathematical communication, not extra prose.

Context

The problem

A bottling process is designed to fill bottles with a mean of 500 mL and a standard deviation of 8 mL. For this exercise, treat individual fill amounts as approximately normal. During an inspection, one bottle contains 516 mL. In a separate sample of 64 bottles, the sample mean is 503 mL with a reported margin of error of 2 mL.
Initial post

What to include

  1. Find the z-score of the 516 mL bottle. Use the Empirical Rule to describe how unusual a value at least this far above the mean would be approximately.
  2. Write the confidence interval determined by 503 ± 2 mL.
  3. Does the process target of 500 mL lie inside that interval?
  4. Explain why the z-score calculation and the confidence interval answer different questions: one concerns an individual observation, while the other concerns a population mean.
Replies

Engage with classmates’ mathematics

A useful reply checks a calculation, compares methods or assumptions, or asks a mathematically specific question. Avoid replies that only say that you agree.
