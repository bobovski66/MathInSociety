How close is close enough?

When would a range of estimates help you make a decision?

EstimatesMarginsDecisions

Start with something familiar

A café reports an estimated average wait of 12 minutes, with a margin of 3 minutes. Subtracting and adding 3 gives an interval from 9 to 15 minutes for the average wait.

That interval is about the café’s average. An individual customer could wait longer or shorter. Whether the estimate is useful also depends on the decision you need to make.

Your post

Choose an average you would find useful: a waiting time, a cost, or time spent on an activity. Imagine a study reports an estimated average and a margin. Make up these two numbers for your example.

Use your own example and answer all the numbered questions. Post your response in Canvas.

  1. What average would you like to know? Describe the people, items, or days it would be about, and why it matters to you.
  2. Choose the reported average and margin. Subtract and add the margin to find the interval. Include the units.
  3. Would that range be close enough for a decision you care about? Explain why or why not.
  4. Would the interval mean every individual value falls inside it? Explain the difference between the average and one person’s or item’s measurement.

Reply to a classmate

Read a classmate’s example. Ask a question, suggest a small change, or connect their example to something you have noticed. Give them something to respond to.

You might start with:

  • “Would you make the same choice if the margin were twice as large?”
  • “What would you want to know about how the study got its numbers? I would wonder about…”

Explain a little of your thinking so your classmate can build on it.
