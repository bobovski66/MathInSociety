When a neighborhood route closes

Some closed routes cause a detour. Others leave people with no way through. Which connections should a community improve first?

A neighborhood connects eight places by roads and walking routes. In the diagram, each letter represents a place, and every line represents a direct route that can be used in both directions. The picture shows which places are connected, not how many miles or minutes apart they are.

The neighborhood network

S — Bus station

L — Library

C — Clinic

T — Transit hub

P — Food pantry

F — Family center

R — Recreation center

H — Senior housing

Follow only the drawn lines. A route can pass through several places, and you can use a different route if a connection is closed.

How to trace a route. From the station S you can reach the clinic C directly in one step, or travel S–L–C in two steps. Count the lines traveled, not the letters in the route.

Your post: answer all four parts

  1. Find two routes. Give a route from the bus station S to senior housing H that uses as few connections as possible. How many lines does it use? Then find a different route from S to H. Count its lines too.
  2. Compare two closures. First imagine that the C–P route is closed. Can someone still travel from S to H? Show a route that works and count its lines. Now restore C–P and close P–H instead. Can anyone reach H from the other locations? Explain why these closures have different results.
  3. Choose an improvement. The neighborhood can afford one new direct connection. Its two proposals are S–P (station to pantry) or F–H (family center to senior housing). Which would you recommend? Consider both a closure of C–P and a closure of P–H. Show at least one route that supports your choice, and explain which problem matters most in your decision.
  4. Bring it into real life. Think of a road, bus route, walking path, or community connection that people in your area depend on. What would happen if it became unavailable? Name one detail our drawing leaves out—such as travel time, cost, accessibility, or who needs the route most—that might change your recommendation.

Write four numbered responses. Use letters such as S–T–F to show a route whenever a question asks for one. Different recommendations in Question 3 are welcome when they are supported with reasoning.

Reply to a classmate

Read a classmate's recommendation. Check one of the routes they used, then compare their proposed link with the other option. What would each choice improve, and what problem would remain? Ask one specific question about who benefits from the improvement.

For example: “You chose S–P because it helps people get across the neighborhood faster. I was thinking about what happens if P–H closes. Would your new route still help someone living at H? How should we weigh those two concerns?”
