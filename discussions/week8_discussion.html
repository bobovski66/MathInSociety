When one route closes

Find a route, see what happens when it closes, and decide which connection a community should improve.

Use the picture below for the first three questions. Each dot is a place, and each line is a direct two-way route. Then think about a connection that matters in your own community.

The neighborhood

A neighborhood has five places: S = bus station, L = library, C = clinic, P = food pantry, and H = senior housing. The lines show usable direct routes. The drawing is schematic: line lengths do not show travel time.

Direct routes: S–L, S–C, L–C, C–P, and P–H.

How to count a route. S–L–C passes through three places but uses only two connecting lines. To find a route, follow lines from one letter to the next without jumping across empty space.

Your post: answer all four parts

  1. Get somewhere. Write a route from the station S to senior housing H. How many connecting lines does it use? Count the lines, not the letters.
  2. Close one route. Imagine C–P is closed for repairs. Which places can S still reach? Can someone at S get to H using the remaining lines? Explain how the picture shows your answer.
  3. Propose a fix. The neighborhood can build one new direct link. Name its two endpoints (for example, write “X–Y,” using actual letters from the diagram). Choose a link that would reconnect the station side to the pantry and senior housing after C–P closes. Explain how it helps.
  4. Make it real. Think of a road, bus service, walking route, communication link, or local partnership that matters to people you know. What happens if it is unavailable? Name one practical issue a network picture would leave out, such as travel time, cost, accessibility, reliability, or the needs of different users.

Write four numbered responses. For the first three, include the letters of the places or the route you used in your reasoning.

Reply to a classmate

Read a classmate's proposed new link. Suggest a different connection or improvement that might also help, and explain one advantage or limitation of your idea compared with theirs. Then ask one specific question about the people who would depend on that connection.

For example: “Your proposed S–P link reconnects the pantry. I would add L–H instead so people at the library could reach senior housing directly. Which route would be more useful to residents?”
