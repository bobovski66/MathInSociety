Week 8 / Discussion

Show what is possible. Explain what matters.

Model a campus print-room decision, check proposed plans, and compare how different priorities change the best feasible choice.

A checkable modelCompeting prioritiesA mathematically useful reply

The situation / A campus print room

Which plans can the room actually produce?

The room is preparing posters and banners for an event. Let x count posters and y count banners. A poster needs 1 unit of printer time and 2 units of finishing time; a banner needs 2 printer units and 1 finishing unit. At most 24 printer-time units and 30 finishing-time units are available.

ResourcePer poster, xPer banner, yAvailable
Printer time1 unit2 units24 units
Finishing time2 units1 unit30 units
\[x+2y\leq24,\qquad2x+y\leq30,\qquad x,y\geq0.\]

The quantities are invented and the rates are constant. Products must be whole, but first draw the continuous region. Three suggested plans are (10,5), (8,9), and (14,3). Passing one resource test is not enough.

Prepare a solution another student can check

  1. Find both intercepts of each boundary line.
  2. Find the intersection of the two non-axis boundaries and check it.
  3. List every feasible vertex, including the axis corners. A sketch or clear vertex table is welcome.
  4. Test each proposed plan against both limits. Identify the violated limit for an infeasible plan.
  5. At the non-axis intersection, state which resource constraints are binding.

Example · A complete resource check for a different plan

At (4,2), printer use is 4+2(2)=8≤24 and finishing use is 2(4)+2=10≤30. Both coordinates are nonnegative, so this plan is feasible. This check establishes possibility; it does not establish optimality for any goal.

Then choose a goal and defend the result

The event might value many posters for broad distribution or many banners for large signs. Choose one of these invented priority scores, or define a different linear score and explain its coefficients:

  • Poster-focused score: maximize S=5x+2y.
  • Banner-focused score: maximize T=2x+7y.

Evaluate your score at every feasible vertex and recommend a plan. Explain how the score represents a priority, rather than treating it as an objective measure of what the event ought to value. Name one real feature the model omits—demand, minimum quantities, accessibility, setup time, changing rates, or uncertainty—and say what information would help.

Your post can use a labeled graph, tables, equations, and a short explanation. The goal is enough evidence for someone else to verify the recommendation, not extra prose.

Draft your post

Your writing saves in this browser. This page does not post to the class discussion board; copy your finished work into the discussion space or submit it as directed.

Reply by changing the question or testing the evidence

Choose a classmate’s result and do one specific check. Recalculate one proposed plan; verify a corner against both limits; or compare their recommended plan under the other priority score. Explain what your check establishes.

For a deeper reply, give the printer 30 units instead of 24 while leaving finishing at 30. Does your classmate’s goal necessarily produce a better score, or might finishing still block improvement? Show a route through the inequalities or a changed corner calculation. More capacity can help, but the objective and remaining constraints determine whether it does.

Before you post

  • I defined x and y and kept the coordinates in the right order.
  • I checked every candidate corner and proposed plan against every constraint.
  • I separated “feasible” from “optimal for my stated score.”
  • I reported a plan and its objective value, with a meaningful interpretation.
  • I explained how an assumption or missing feature could affect the decision.

The mathematical region makes tradeoffs visible. Choosing the priority is itself part of the modeling conversation.
