Making room for what matters

A community group has limited time and money. How should it decide what to do first?

The situation

A neighborhood volunteer group has three hours and $20 available for a Saturday morning. It would like to do two things: clean up a public walking trail (two volunteer-hours and $12 for bags and gloves) and deliver groceries to a few neighbors (one volunteer-hour and $9 for fuel). Volunteers are available for either activity, but the group must decide what fits its time and money.

Both activities would benefit the neighborhood, and the group would like to do as much as it can. A plan is feasible only if it fits the time and budget available. The group can also consider whether a small change to its plan would help.

Your post

Use the group's time and budget to decide which activities are possible, and explain what you would prioritize.

Post your answers in Canvas.

  1. Calculate the total volunteer time and cost of doing both activities. Which limit, if any, prevents the group from doing everything it planned?
  2. Imagine you are helping the group decide. Would you prioritize the trail cleanup, grocery delivery, or a modified plan? Explain which community needs or personal experiences inform your choice.
  3. Suppose the group could get one extra volunteer-hour or $2 more in donations. Which would make a difference to its options? Use the numbers to explain, then describe another change the group might consider.

Reply to a classmate

Reply to a classmate's proposed plan. Ask about a tradeoff they considered, explain how their priorities compare with yours, or suggest a way to adjust the plan while respecting both limits.
