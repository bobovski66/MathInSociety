Module 8 Discussion — The Shape of a Feasible Region

Turn production limits into half-planes and test proposed plans.

How this discussion works

Solve, present, then respond

Work the problem below before reading classmates’ solutions. In your initial post, show enough mathematical work that another student can follow your method, then state the result in the language of the situation. Post your solution in the class discussion board; this page itself does not submit responses.

A clear table, calculation, labeled graph, or photo of neatly organized handwritten work is fine when appropriate. The goal is mathematical communication, not extra prose.

Context

The problem

A school print room produces posters \(x\) and banners \(y\) for an event. A large-format printer imposes the constraint \(x+2y\le 24\). Finishing time imposes \(2x+y\le 30\). Production cannot be negative, so \(x\ge0\) and \(y\ge0\).
Initial post

What to include

  1. Find the x- and y-intercepts of both boundary lines.
  2. Sketch the two half-planes in the first quadrant and identify their intersection as the feasible region.
  3. Find all vertices of the feasible polygon, including the intersection of the two boundary lines.
  4. Test each proposed plan by substitution: (10,5), (8,9), and (14,3). State which are feasible.
  5. At the intersection vertex, identify which constraints are active (satisfied with equality).
Replies

Engage with classmates’ mathematics

Compare your vertex list or sketch with a classmate’s. Verify one proposed plan from their post by direct substitution, and explain how one of the inequalities creates a particular edge of the feasible region.

A useful reply checks a calculation, compares methods or assumptions, or asks a mathematically specific question. Avoid replies that only say that you agree.
