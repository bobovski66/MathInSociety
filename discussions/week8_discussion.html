Making room for what matters

How do you choose when your time and money will only go so far?

LimitsPossible plansGoals

Start with something familiar

You have 3 hours and $20 for an afternoon project. Baking takes 2 hours and costs $12. Making a card takes 1 hour and costs $5. Doing them one after the other uses 3 hours and $17, so it fits both limits.

Another plan might also fit. Choosing between possible plans means thinking about what you want from the afternoon.

Your post

Plan something you would enjoy or find useful: a day off, a club event, or a small project. Choose two activities or items. Estimate the time and money each needs, then set your available time and money. Made-up amounts are fine.

Use your own example and answer all the numbered questions. Post your response in Canvas.

  1. What are you planning? State your two choices, their costs and times, and your time and money limits.
  2. Make two plans you could consider. Add up the time and money for each plan. Which ones fit both limits? Show your totals.
  3. Which plan would you choose, or how would you change one to make it work? What are you trying to get more of or use less of, and why does that matter to you?
  4. If you could increase just one limit—time or money—which would you choose? Give it a larger amount and check whether that would change what you can do.

Reply to a classmate

Read a classmate’s example. Ask a question, suggest a small change, or connect their example to something you have noticed. Give them something to respond to.

You might start with:

  • “Would your choice change if you had one more hour?”
  • “Could you make room for ___ by doing less of ___? I wonder whether…”

Explain a little of your thinking so your classmate can build on it.
