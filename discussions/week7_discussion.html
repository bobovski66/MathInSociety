Module 7 Discussion — Pricing a Pop-Up Shop

Use cost, revenue, profit, and break-even to compare pricing assumptions.

How this discussion works

Solve, present, then respond

Work the problem below before reading classmates’ solutions. In your initial post, show enough mathematical work that another student can follow your method, then state the result in the language of the situation. Post your solution in the class discussion board; this page itself does not submit responses.

A clear table, calculation, labeled graph, or photo of neatly organized handwritten work is fine when appropriate. The goal is mathematical communication, not extra prose.

Context

The problem

A student club is planning a one-day T-shirt booth to raise money for an upcoming trip. Renting the booth, printing signs, and other setup expenses create a fixed cost of $300. Each shirt then costs the club $6 to purchase and package.

Before the event, the club must choose a selling price. Members are considering $12, $14, or $16 per shirt. To isolate the relationship among cost, revenue, profit, and break-even, use a simplified model in which every shirt offered for sale is sold and the $6 per-shirt cost remains constant.

Choose one selling price to analyze. Your job is to build the cost, revenue, and profit models, determine how many sales are needed to cover the fixed cost, and then interpret what the model does—and does not—tell the club about its pricing decision.

Initial post

What to include

  1. Write the cost function \(C(q)\), revenue function \(R(q)\), and profit function \(P(q)\) for your chosen price.
  2. Find the break-even quantity. If the result is not a whole number, state the smallest whole number of shirts that avoids a loss.
  3. Compute the profit if 60 shirts are sold.
  4. Find the marginal profit per additional shirt.
  5. Explain one important limitation of comparing the three prices while holding the number sold fixed.
Replies

Engage with classmates’ mathematics

Compare your price choice with a classmate who used a different price. Check their break-even calculation, then explain how changing price affects both marginal profit and break-even under the model’s fixed-sales assumption.

A useful reply checks a calculation, compares methods or assumptions, or asks a mathematically specific question. Avoid replies that only say that you agree.
