Week 2 / Discussion

Who gets asked?

How could the people you ask change the answer you get?

An example

You ask 10 people at a library whether they prefer printed books. Eight say yes. That is 8 ÷ 10 × 100 = 80% of the people you asked.

A survey outside the library might give a different result because it reaches different people.

Your post

Think of a yes-or-no question you would like answered about your class, neighborhood, workplace, or a hobby. Make up a small survey result for this discussion.

Answer the four questions using your example, then post your response in Canvas.

  1. What would you ask, and whose opinions do you want to learn about?
  2. Where or how would you ask? Imagine getting 10 answers. Choose how many say yes, then calculate that percentage: number of yes answers ÷ 10 × 100.
  3. Who might be left out by the way you ask? How could you hear from them?
  4. Would these answers be enough to help you make a decision? Explain what you would still want to know.

Reply to a classmate

Read a classmate's example and respond with a question, suggestion, or related experience.

You might start with:

  • “Would you get different answers if you asked ___?”
  • “How could you hear from people who ___? Their answers might matter because…”

Include a sentence about your reasoning.
