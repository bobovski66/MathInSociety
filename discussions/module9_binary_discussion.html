Which requests should be easiest to reach?

A community center is redesigning its phone menu. Your task is to recommend an arrangement and explain who it helps.

Twenty calls, four services

The center receives ten calls for bus schedules, five for food pantry hours, three for clinic appointments, and two for shelter information. Every caller must reach one of the four services.

How to count. If ten bus callers each receive a one-digit code, they answer 10 × 1 = 10 questions together. If the bus code is three digits long, they answer 10 × 3 = 30 questions.

Your post: answer all four parts

  1. Assign the codes. Give each service one of the four codes. Which service receives 0? Explain your decision.
  2. Count the work. Multiply the calls for each service by the length of its code. Add your four results. How does that compare with a menu asking all 20 callers exactly two questions?
  3. Consider urgency. Someone seeking shelter may need immediate help. Would you change your codes, even if that increases the total number of questions? Why?
  4. Use your experience. Describe a phone menu, online form or similar system you have used. What made it easy or difficult? Name one concern this simplified calculation leaves out.

Reply to a classmate

Find a classmate who assigned the shortest code differently or made a different recommendation about urgent requests. Explain one advantage of their design and one advantage of yours, then ask a specific question about the needs of someone using the menu.
