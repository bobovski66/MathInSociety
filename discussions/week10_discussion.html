How far would you trust a prediction?

When two predictions suggest different decisions, what should we do?

The situation

A community center is planning a free workshop with room for 50 participants. Early registration reports differ slightly: one person counted 5 interested participants, while another counted 6. The center tries a simple rule for estimating how interest might spread: next week’s total = twice this week’s total + 1.

This is only a planning exercise. Interest might spread by word of mouth for a little while, but real registration depends on schedules, outreach, capacity, and whether interested people actually attend. The center wants to know whether it should prepare for a full room or take another count.

Your post

Calculate both forecasts, then decide how you would plan when the uncertainty affects a real capacity limit.

Post your answers in Canvas.

  1. Calculate three weekly updates from starts 5 and 6 using the same rule. How many interested participants does each forecast predict after three weeks, and how large is the gap?
  2. One forecast is below the 50-seat limit and the other is above it. How would you advise the center to plan? Explain what you would prioritize, drawing on any experience with sign-ups, crowded events, or uncertain plans.
  3. What new information would you gather before making the decision? When might this simple doubling rule stop being useful?

Reply to a classmate

Reply to a classmate's recommendation. Consider how their plan handles an unexpected crowd or empty seats, suggest another piece of evidence to collect, or explain how a different starting estimate might change the choice.
