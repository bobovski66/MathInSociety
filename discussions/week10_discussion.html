How far would you trust a prediction?

What can a repeated rule tell you—and what would you still need to find out?

Repeated rulesStarting numbersForecasts

Start with something familiar

We predict things when we plan a trip, practice a skill, or expect a project to grow. A number can help us think ahead, but we also need to ask where it came from.

This week’s activities show that repeating a clear rule can lead to surprising results. Here, try a simple rule for something you know and consider how much you would trust it.

One short example

A made-up forecast doubles an amount each round. Starting with 5 and starting with 6 gives:

StartAfter 1 roundAfter 2After 3
5102040
6122448

The starting gap is 1; after three rounds it is 8. This doubling rule still has a predictable pattern.

Your post

Choose something you might try to predict, such as practice time, progress on a project, or the number of people joining an activity. Make up a simple rule that could fit it: add a fixed amount, multiply by the same number, or take half of what is left.

Use your own example and answer all the numbered questions. Post your response in Canvas.

  1. What are you trying to predict, and why would the prediction matter to you? State your starting number, rule, and what one step means.
  2. Use your rule for three steps. Then change the starting number a little and repeat the same three steps. Show both sets of numbers and the difference between their final values.
  3. What might happen in your real example that your rule leaves out? Would the same pattern keep working? Explain with a specific possibility.
  4. How far ahead would you trust this forecast, and why? What would you check as new information arrives?

Reply to a classmate

Read a classmate’s example. Ask a question, suggest a small change, or connect their example to something you have noticed. Give them something to respond to.

You might start with:

  • “Would changing the starting number by ___ change your decision?”
  • “What new information might make you revise the rule? I would watch for…”

Explain a little of your thinking so your classmate can build on it.
