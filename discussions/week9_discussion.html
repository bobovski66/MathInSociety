Connections that matter

What happens to access when a familiar route closes?

The situation

A neighborhood has an apartment building (A), a bus stop (B), a library (C), and a clinic (D). For this simplified walking-path map, direct pedestrian connections are A–B, B–C, C–D, and B–D. Assume all four paths are open to pedestrians in either direction.

Residents of the apartment building can currently walk to the clinic through the bus stop. Now imagine that the B–D footpath is closed for repairs. The map tells us how many path segments are needed; it does not yet tell us the walking time or whether each path is accessible.

Your post

Compare the paths before and after the closure, then consider the people who depend on them.

Post your answers in Canvas.

  1. From the apartment building (A) to the clinic (D), give a shortest route and count its walking-path segments. After B–D closes, give the new shortest route and its number of segments.
  2. Which residents might be most affected by the closure? Draw on an experience with transportation, access to services, or a route you know, and explain why the number of segments might miss something important.
  3. Suggest one additional pedestrian link that would improve access for a particular purpose. Explain which route it creates and what information you would want before recommending construction.

Reply to a classmate

Reply to a classmate's proposed improvement. Ask about whose needs it serves, offer a different consideration such as walking time or accessibility, or explain how a change in one route might alter their recommendation.
