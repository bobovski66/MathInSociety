Connections that matter

What could one changed connection mean in a network you know?

NetworksRoutesConnections

Start with something familiar

Suppose A passes a message to B, B to C, and C to D. Getting from A to D takes three connections. If A and D can contact each other directly, that route takes just one connection: two fewer.

We can use the same kind of drawing for routes between places, contacts in a group, or connections between devices. The meaning of a useful change depends on the situation.

Your post

Choose a network from your life or interests: roads, a game group, a workplace, a club, or something else. Sketch four to six points and the connections between them. Label the points A, B, C, and so on. For this drawing, treat each connection as working in both directions.

Use your own example and answer all the numbered questions. Post your response in Canvas.

  1. What do your points and connections stand for? Why does this network matter to you? Include your sketch or describe the connections in words.
  2. Choose two points that are connected by a route. Find the route using the fewest connections. Show it and count the connections, rather than the points.
  3. Add one connection or remove one. Check the route between the same two points again. How many connections are needed now, or is there no route? What could that change mean for the people using the network?
  4. What would it take to make that change in real life? What important detail might your drawing leave out?

Reply to a classmate

Read a classmate’s example. Ask a question, suggest a small change, or connect their example to something you have noticed. Give them something to respond to.

You might start with:

  • “What would happen if the connection between ___ and ___ stopped working?”
  • “Could a connection from ___ to ___ help in a different way? I am thinking about…”

Explain a little of your thinking so your classmate can build on it.
