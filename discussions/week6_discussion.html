A payment that works for you

How would you balance paying something off with the other things you need money for?

Start with something familiar

Suppose you owe $400. In a made-up loan, the monthly interest is 1%, so the first month adds $400 × 0.01 = $4. A $50 payment then leaves $400 + $4 − $50 = $354 owed.

A larger payment reduces the debt faster, while a smaller payment leaves more room in the monthly budget. The better choice depends on the borrower's other needs.

Your post

Imagine borrowing for a repair, a useful item, a class, or another project you care about. Choose a starting amount and two possible monthly payments. You can invent all the amounts. Use the same made-up monthly interest rate of 1%. Add interest first, then take off the payment.

Answer the following questions using your example, then post your response in Canvas.

  1. What would the borrowed money help you do? How much would you start out owing?
  2. Find the first month’s interest: starting amount × 0.01. For each payment, calculate starting amount + interest − payment. Choose payments smaller than your starting balance, and round money to cents.
  3. Which payment would you choose, and why? What else would you need to consider that month?
  4. In this model, next month’s interest is 1% of the balance left. Which payment would lead to less interest next month? Explain how the changing balance makes that happen.

Reply to a classmate

Read a classmate’s example. Ask a question, suggest a small change, or connect their example to something you have noticed. Give them something to respond to.

You might start with:

  • “What would happen to the balance if you paid $10 more each month?”
  • “What other cost might affect which payment you choose? I am thinking about…”

Give a reason or example that helps your classmate continue the conversation.
