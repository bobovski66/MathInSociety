Module 6 Discussion — How Payment Size Changes a Loan

Compare debt repayment using a monthly recurrence.

How this discussion works

Solve, present, then respond

Work the problem below before reading classmates’ solutions. In your initial post, show enough mathematical work that another student can follow your method, then state the result in the language of the situation. Post your solution in the class discussion board; this page itself does not submit responses.

A clear table, calculation, labeled graph, or photo of neatly organized handwritten work is fine when appropriate. The goal is mathematical communication, not extra prose.

Context

The problem

A credit-card balance is $5,000 with an APR of 18%, compounded monthly. Assume no new purchases, fees, or rate changes. Interest is added first each month, then the payment is made. Choose one monthly payment: $150, $225, or $300. Use the monthly rate \(0.18/12=0.015\).
Initial post

What to include

  1. Write the monthly balance recurrence for your chosen payment.
  2. Compute the interest, principal reduction, and ending balance for each of the first three months. Round money to the nearest cent each month.
  3. Use the Interest & Loan Explorer from Module 6 to estimate the number of months required to pay off the balance and the total interest paid.
  4. Explain why all three payment plans begin with the same first-month interest but produce different later interest charges.
  5. State one mathematical tradeoff involved in choosing a larger monthly payment.
Replies

Engage with classmates’ mathematics

Respond to someone who chose a different payment if possible. Compare payoff time and total interest, and verify that the difference is consistent with the recurrence rather than simply saying that the larger payment is “better.”

A useful reply checks a calculation, compares methods or assumptions, or asks a mathematically specific question. Avoid replies that only say that you agree.
