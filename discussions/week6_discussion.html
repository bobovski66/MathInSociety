A payment changes more than one month.

Build three loan updates, verify a complete schedule, and explain how payment size shapes later balances and interest.

The task / Follow a payment through the next balance

Show how a monthly payment changes later interest

A hypothetical borrower has a $5,000 balance at a fixed 18% nominal annual rate. Use monthly interest i=0.18/12=0.015. Interest is added first, then the payment is applied. Assume no new purchases, fees, or rate changes.

Choose a fixed monthly payment of $150, $225, or $300 to analyze in detail. These are model choices, not statements about what anyone can afford. Your comparison should show both the monthly commitment and its consequences over time.

Work before reading classmates’ posts. Use a table, labeled calculations, graph, or organized handwritten work to communicate your reasoning. The fields below save drafts in this browser; post the finished work through your class discussion board as directed.

Part A / Build the first three months by hand

Use the new balance each time

Write \(B_{k+1}=1.015B_k-M\) with your chosen M and starting value \(B_0=5000\). For this discussion, round each month’s interest to the nearest cent before calculating the end balance.

Part B / Follow the whole schedule

Connect the hand calculation to the explorer

Open the loan explorer and choose the discussion preset. Enter your selected payment, keep monthly cent rounding, and use a long enough horizon to reach payoff. Compare the first three rows with your hand work before recording the totals.

  1. Record the payoff month, actual last payment, total paid, and total interest.
  2. Verify total interest equals total paid minus the $5,000 starting principal.
  3. Explain why all three choices have the same first interest charge.
  4. Use a later starting balance to explain why their subsequent interest charges differ.
  5. Describe the tradeoff between monthly payment size and the duration and cost of this modeled debt.

Reply by testing a mathematical claim

Compare with a classmate’s payment choice, or investigate a second amount yourself. Check one of their monthly rows, then compare payoff time and total interest under the same rounding convention. Explain the difference through the changing balances.

For another specific test, consider a $75 payment at the original $5,000 balance. Predict whether the debt initially grows, stays fixed, or shrinks. Use that prediction to explain why “a payment was made” does not by itself imply progress toward payoff under a stated interest rule.

Review before posting

  • I distinguished interest, actual payment, and principal reduction.
  • I used monthly cent rounding for both hand work and the explorer.
  • I reported a payoff only after the balance reached zero.
  • My comparison states its assumptions and contributes mathematical reasoning.
