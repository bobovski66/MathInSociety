What does an average miss?

Does a typical number tell you what you need to know?

Start with something familiar

Three trips take 20, 25, and 30 minutes. Their mean, or average, is (20 + 25 + 30) ÷ 3 = 25 minutes. Their range is 30 − 20 = 10 minutes.

The average is useful, but you might also care about the longest trip—especially if you need to arrive on time.

Your post

Choose something in your day that varies: travel time, waiting time, practice time, or another measurement you care about. Use three to five values. They can be real or made up.

Use your own example and answer all the numbered questions. Post your response in Canvas.

  1. What do your values measure, and why do you care about them? Include the units, such as minutes or dollars.
  2. List your values. Find the mean by adding them and dividing by how many there are. Find the range by subtracting the smallest from the largest.
  3. Does the average give a useful picture of your example? Use one of your values to explain what it might miss.
  4. Would you want the values to stay close together, or would some variety be welcome? Explain how that would affect you.

Reply to a classmate

Read a classmate’s example. Ask a question, suggest a small change, or connect their example to something you have noticed. Give them something to respond to.

You might start with:

  • “Would the average still be helpful if one day ___?”
  • “I would care most about ___ in your example because… What do you think?”

Explain a little of your thinking so your classmate can build on it.
