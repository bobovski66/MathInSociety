An idea that changed cybernetics

Margaret Mead and the observer inside the system

Cybernetics is often introduced through engineering: a thermostat senses the room, compares the measurement with a target, and adjusts the heat. Margaret Mead asked what happens when the system is a group of people—including the people doing the observing.

As an anthropologist and participant in the Macy Conferences of 1946–1953, Mead helped bring questions about communication, culture, language, and social organization into cybernetics. The meetings were not only about machines: they were also about how living and social systems respond to information.

In a 1967 address published in 1968 as Cybernetics of Cybernetics, Mead suggested that the American Society for Cybernetics should examine its own operation through cybernetic principles. In other words: could the people studying feedback use feedback to govern how they worked together?

Consider a classroom

A teacher changes instruction after a quiz. Students notice the change and alter their studying. The teacher then sees different quiz results. Who is observing whom? Does the observer's decision become part of what is observed next?

About this page

Compiled for MATH& 107 · Math in Society. This is a selected teaching timeline, not an exhaustive history. Entries simplify advanced work to emphasize the ideas and questions that students can meaningfully explore. Contemporary entries may describe active research rather than a single invention.

The early probability and entropy developments predate the names information theory and cybernetics; they are included as mathematical precursors, not as instances of those later disciplines.
