Module 6: AI Application — Exponential Growth and AI Scaling

Repeated percentage growth in computing capacity and data demand.

Why this page exists

Mathematics gives us a way into AI

AI systems are often discussed using enormous numbers: parameters, tokens, processors, and computing operations. You do not need those technical details to study an important mathematical feature of scaling: repeated percentage growth can become very large very quickly.

Important: the examples on this page are intentionally tiny models. Real LLMs use huge vectors, many layers, and billions of learned numerical parameters. We simplify the scale while keeping the mathematical structure recognizable.

Connection to this module

The mathematics you already know

Module 6 compares additive growth with multiplicative growth. The same distinction appears whenever a technology budget, data collection program, or computing resource grows by a fixed percentage each period.

AI vocabulary

Four ideas to carry with you

computeA broad term for the numerical work performed by computer hardware.
scalingIncreasing the size of a model, dataset, or computation.
linear growthAdding the same amount each period.
exponential growthMultiplying by the same factor each period.
Worked example

Follow the numbers

Imagine a research group begins with 100 units of computing capacity. If it adds 25 units per year, \[ C_n=100+25n. \] If instead capacity grows by 25% per year, \[ C_n=100(1.25)^n. \] After 4 years the linear model gives 200 units, while the multiplicative model gives about \[ 100(1.25)^4\approx244.14. \] The two stories diverge even though both begin with “25.”
Investigation

Try the mathematics yourself

1. Starting from 100 units and adding 25 units each year, how many units are available after 4 years?

2. Starting from 100 units and growing 25% per year, how many units are available after 2 years?

3. Using the Rule of 72, about how many years would a quantity take to double at 12% annual growth?

4. Which growth rule eventually increases faster?

What to remember

AI idea of the week

The social impact of a technology can change rapidly when important resources scale multiplicatively. Mathematics helps separate “large” from “growing quickly,” which are not the same claim.
