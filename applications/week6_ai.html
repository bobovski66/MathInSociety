Module 6: AI Application — Growth, Scale, and AI

Additive and multiplicative growth help us reason about rapidly changing computational systems.

A question for this module
When people say AI is 'scaling quickly,' what kind of growth are they talking about?

Large numbers are easy to make impressive and hard to interpret. A useful first question is whether a quantity is growing by a fixed amount or by a fixed percentage. Those are different mathematical stories.

Start with familiar mathematics

The Module 6 idea

Start with 100 units. If we add 25 each year, \[ A_n=100+25n. \] If instead we grow by 25% each year, \[ M_n=100(1.25)^n. \] After four years, \[ A_4=200, \] but \[ M_4\approx244.14. \] Compounding causes the gap to widen.
Now put that mathematics to work

The AI connection

AI systems involve quantities such as computing capacity, data processed, electricity used, users served, and money spent. A statement such as “capacity increased by 25 units” describes additive growth. “Capacity increased by 25%” describes multiplicative growth. For this application, use a hypothetical AI computing center. The numbers are invented so that we can focus on the mathematics rather than on rapidly changing industry statistics.
Keep the scale in perspective. These numbers are deliberately small so the calculation is visible. A real LLM uses a much larger vocabulary, high-dimensional vectors, and many repeated layers. We are isolating one genuine mathematical structure at a time.
Follow one example

Work through the calculation

A lab starts with 100 compute units. Under an additive plan, \[ A_n=100+25n. \] After two years, \[ A_2=150. \] Under a 25% annual growth plan, \[ B_n=100(1.25)^n. \] After two years, \[ B_2=156.25. \] Both plans began with “25,” but one meant 25 units and the other meant 25 percent.

Where this leaves us
When a technology is changing quickly, “how much?” is only half the question. “By what rule?” matters just as much. Module 6 gives you a way to separate a large number from a rapidly compounding one.
