Module 6: AI Application — Growth, Scale, and AI

Use the same growth lens as the interest models: distinguish fixed additions from percentage growth applied to the current state, then ask what limits or actions might change the trajectory.

A question for this module
When a computing resource grows, what rule is changing its current state?

A growing number alone does not show how the system changes. A fixed addition is an additive update. A fixed percentage applied to the current amount is a multiplicative update. The second can amplify earlier growth, as compound interest does.

Start with familiar mathematics

The Module 6 idea

Start with 100 units. If we add 25 each year, \[ A_n=100+25n. \] If instead we grow by 25% each year, \[ M_n=100(1.25)^n. \] After four years, \[ A_4=200, \] but \[ M_4\approx244.14. \] Compounding makes each year's increase depend on the amount reached so far, so the difference between the two paths widens.
Now put that mathematics to work

The AI connection

AI systems involve quantities such as computing capacity, data processed, electricity used, users served, and money spent. A statement such as “capacity increased by 25 units” describes an additive update. “Capacity increased by 25%” describes a multiplicative update whose next increase depends on the current level. Use the hypothetical computing center below to practice the comparison. The numbers are invented so the calculation stays visible; they do not claim that real AI capacity follows one fixed growth rate. In real systems, energy, money, equipment, and demand can limit or redirect growth.
Keep the scale in perspective. These numbers are deliberately small so the calculation is visible. A real LLM uses a much larger vocabulary, high-dimensional vectors, and many repeated layers. We are isolating one genuine mathematical structure at a time.
Follow one example

Work through the calculation

A lab starts with 100 compute units. Under an additive plan, \[ A_n=100+25n. \] After two years, \[ A_2=150. \] Under a 25% annual growth plan, \[ B_n=100(1.25)^n. \] After two years, \[ B_2=156.25. \] Both plans use the number 25, but one adds 25 units while the other multiplies the current amount by 1.25. The percentage-growth path is an amplifying process under the stated assumption that the 25% rate continues.

Where this leaves us
When a technology changes, ask both how much and by what rule. Compound growth describes an amplifying update; resource limits and decisions can change that update. The same questions will help us study feedback and constraints in later modules.
