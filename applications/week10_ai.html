Module 10: AI Application — Autoregression, Branching, and Prediction

Why one small early choice can send generated text down a very different path.

Why this page exists

Mathematics gives us a way into AI

An LLM is called autoregressive when it generates a sequence one step at a time and feeds the growing sequence back into the next prediction. The process is recursive, but unlike the logistic map it also commonly includes probabilistic sampling.

Important: the examples on this page are intentionally tiny models. Real LLMs use huge vectors, many layers, and billions of learned numerical parameters. We simplify the scale while keeping the mathematical structure recognizable.

Connection to this module

The mathematics you already know

Module 10 studies recursion and limits of prediction. Text generation offers a vivid example: a tiny change in an early token can change all the probability distributions that come afterward.

AI vocabulary

Four ideas to carry with you

autoregressiveGenerated values are conditioned on previously generated values.
trajectoryOne complete path through a sequence of generated tokens.
branchingDifferent possible next tokens lead to different future contexts.
hallucinationA fluent model output that contains unsupported or false content; probabilistic fluency does not guarantee factual truth.
Worked example

Follow the numbers

Suppose the prompt is “The light in the forest was …” and the next-token distribution is \[ P(\text{moving})=.45,\quad P(\text{flickering})=.35,\quad P(\text{Venus})=.20. \] If “moving” is selected, the next distribution is computed from a context ending in “moving.” If “Venus” is selected, the next distribution is computed from a different context. One early branch changes the entire later trajectory.

What to remember

AI idea of the week

Autoregressive generation is a dynamical process through a vast space of possible text sequences. Probability shapes each local step; recursion turns those local choices into a complete response.

You do not need to know every engineering detail of an LLM to reason mathematically about what it is doing. We will keep adding one mathematical layer at a time.
