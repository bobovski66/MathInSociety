AI Mathematics 5 — Surprise, Information, and Cross-Entropy

Why predicting text well is mathematically connected to bits, compression, and the average surprise of the tokens that actually occur.

Why this belongs in Math in Society

Information theory gives us a direct mathematical way to score predictions.

If a model says an event is very likely and it occurs, the event is not surprising. If the model says an event is extremely unlikely and it occurs anyway, the event carries more information relative to that prediction.

For probability (p), the information or surprisal is (I=-log_2 p) bits. Conveniently, when (p=1/2^k), the surprise is exactly (k) bits. During language-model training, we can average this surprise over the correct next tokens. That average is closely related to cross-entropy loss.

What is simplified here? Real training averages token losses over enormous datasets and uses natural logarithms or equivalent conventions in software. The base of the logarithm changes the units, not the underlying idea. Good prediction is closely connected to efficient coding, but a deployed LLM does much more than literal file compression.

If the correct next tokens in a short training example receive surprises (I_1,ldots,I_n), then the average surprise is (H=(I_1+cdots+I_n)/n). A common related quantity is perplexity (2^H).
