Week 7 Application — A Pop-Up Food Stand

Use cost, revenue, profit, and break-even models to decide how many meals a one-day food stand must sell.

How to complete this application

  1. Define fixed cost, variable cost, selling price, and quantity from the story.
  2. Write \(C(q)\), \(R(q)\), and \(\pi(q)\) before calculating break-even.
  3. Use marginal profit to compare what one additional sale contributes under each price.

Math model

\(C(q)=F+vq\), \(R(q)=pq\), \(\pi(q)=(p-v)q-F\), and \(q^*=F/(p-v)\).

Context

The business model

A student organization operates a food stand at a community festival. The organization pays $240 for the booth permit, equipment rental, and other costs that do not depend on the number of meals sold. Each meal costs $3.50 for ingredients and packaging. Each meal sells for $9.50.

Fixed cost

F = $240

Paid even if no meals are sold.

Variable cost

v = $3.50/meal

Added for each meal prepared.

Price

p = $9.50/meal

Revenue received for each meal sold.

Assumption for this model: every meal prepared is sold, and the fixed cost, variable cost, and selling price stay constant over the range considered.
