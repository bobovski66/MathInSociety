Week 2 Application — Community Transportation Survey

Use a sample to build an empirical probability distribution for commuting methods.

How to complete this application

  1. Identify the population and the observed sample before converting any counts.
  2. Divide each count by 80 to obtain the empirical distribution and verify that the proportions total 1.
  3. Use the bus-station alternative to decide how the sampling location could bias the estimate.

Math model

Relative frequency is \(f_i/n\). The sample proportions estimate population proportions; changing the sampling method can change those estimates even when the arithmetic is correct.

Context and model

A county transportation office wants to estimate how working residents in the county usually travel to work. Surveying every worker is too expensive, so the office selects a sample of 80 working residents from addresses chosen at random across several neighborhoods.

The survey records one commuting method for each person:

MethodCount
Drive38
Public transit18
Bike10
Walk14
Total80

The sample counts are observed data. Dividing each count by 80 produces an empirical probability distribution. The office uses those sample proportions as estimates of the corresponding proportions in the full county population.

Investigation

1. Identify population and sample.

2. Convert the counts to relative frequencies.

3. Use the empirical distribution.

4. Sampling method matters.

Suppose instead the office surveyed 80 people while they were waiting at the downtown bus station.
