Module 1: AI Application — How Text Becomes Numbers

Tokens, token IDs, and the recursive loop behind text generation.

Why this page exists

Mathematics gives us a way into AI

Large language models do not begin with words as ideas. They begin with a sequence of symbols called tokens. A token may be a whole word, part of a word, punctuation, or another text fragment. Each token is assigned a number so a computer can store and process it.

Important: the examples on this page are intentionally tiny models. Real LLMs use huge vectors, many layers, and billions of learned numerical parameters. We simplify the scale while keeping the mathematical structure recognizable.

Connection to this module

The mathematics you already know

This fits Module 1 because a tokenized sentence becomes a sequence of numbers, and text generation becomes a recursion: the model uses the current sequence to produce a probability distribution for the next token, selects one token, appends it, and repeats.

AI vocabulary

Four ideas to carry with you

tokenA piece of text represented as one item.
token IDAn integer used to label a token.
vocabularyThe complete list of tokens the model can use.
recursionA process in which each new step is built from the current state.
Worked example

Follow the numbers

Suppose a tiny vocabulary assigns 42 = cat, 17 = sat, 8 = on, and 91 = mat. Then “cat sat on mat” can be stored as the integer sequence \[ (42,17,8,91). \] The numbers themselves do not mean “cat” or “sat”; they are labels. If the model generates one new token at a time, we can describe the process schematically as \[ S_{n+1}=S_n+\text{one new token}, \] where \(S_n\) is the text-so-far.
Investigation

Try the mathematics yourself

1. A toy vocabulary contains 256 possible tokens. How many different integer labels are needed?

2. If the token IDs for “mysterious lights” are 31 and 74, which sequence represents the phrase?

3. A prompt begins with 8 tokens. The model generates 5 more tokens, one per recursive step. How many tokens are in the new sequence?

4. Which description is closest to the generation loop?

What to remember

AI idea of the week

The first mathematical move in an LLM is representation: text becomes a finite sequence of numbered symbols. Once language has become a sequence, recursive mathematics can act on it.

You do not need to know every engineering detail of an LLM to reason mathematically about what it is doing. We will keep adding one mathematical layer at a time.
