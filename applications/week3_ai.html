Module 3: AI Application — Attention as a Weighted Average

A familiar statistical calculation gives us a simplified view of attention.

A question for this module
How can some earlier parts of a sentence matter more than others?

When you read a sentence, not every earlier word matters equally for every later word. A language model also needs a way to combine information from the context unevenly. The mathematics of weighted averages gives us a useful first picture.

Start with familiar mathematics

The Module 3 idea

For the values \[ 2,5,8,10, \] the ordinary mean is \[ \bar x=\frac{2+5+8+10}{4}=6.25. \] You can also write that same mean as \[ 0.25(2)+0.25(5)+0.25(8)+0.25(10)=6.25. \] This makes something visible: an ordinary mean is a weighted average in which every value gets the same weight.
Now put that mathematics to work

The AI connection

Now let the weights be unequal. Imagine four earlier tokens are represented, for one simplified numerical feature, by the values \[ 2,\;5,\;8,\;10. \] Suppose the current calculation gives them weights \[ 0.10,\;0.20,\;0.60,\;0.10. \] The weights are nonnegative and add to 1. In transformer models, numbers of this kind are called attention weights. They allow some earlier information to contribute more strongly than other information. Real attention works with vectors containing many coordinates, not one-number “features.” The one-number version lets us see the weighted-average structure clearly.
Keep the scale in perspective. These numbers are deliberately small so the calculation is visible. A real LLM uses a much larger vocabulary, high-dimensional vectors, and many repeated layers. We are isolating one genuine mathematical structure at a time.
Follow one example

Work through the calculation

Compute the weighted combination: \[ 0.10(2)+0.20(5)+0.60(8)+0.10(10). \] Term by term, \[ 0.20+1.00+4.80+1.00=7.00. \] The result is 7. The value 8 has especially strong influence because it received the largest weight, 0.60. Compare that with the ordinary mean, 6.25. Changing the weights changed the combined result.
Your turn

Try the same idea

Add the attention weights 0.10, 0.20, 0.60, and 0.10.

Compute 0.10(2)+0.20(5)+0.60(8)+0.10(10).

If all four weights were 0.25, what result would you get from 2, 5, 8, 10?

Why does the value 8 have especially strong influence in the weighted calculation?

Where this leaves us
At this level, a good mathematical picture of attention is: \[ \text{earlier information}\longrightarrow\text{context-dependent weighted combination}. \] Later, in Module 8, the same weights will reappear as a geometric idea.
