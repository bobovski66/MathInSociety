Week 10 Application — Fish Population Forecast

Use the logistic map to see how a small measurement uncertainty can become a large long-range forecast difference.

How to complete this application

  1. Use the same logistic rule for both starting measurements so only the initial condition changes.
  2. Compare the two forecasts generation by generation and identify when their difference becomes operationally large.
  3. Interpret the final difference in fish, not just as a difference in normalized \(x\)-values.

Math model

The logistic map is \(x_{n+1}=rx_n(1-x_n)\). If carrying capacity is \(K\), the corresponding population is \(Kx_n\). In a chaotic regime, tiny initial differences can grow dramatically.

Scenario

A lake near carrying capacity

A fisheries team models a fish population as a fraction of the lake's carrying capacity, taken to be 100,000 fish. Their model is

xₙ₊₁ = 3.9 xₙ(1−xₙ)

The survey reports x₀=0.4000, or 40% of carrying capacity. Because field measurements are not exact, x₀=0.4001 is also consistent with the measurement. The two initial populations differ by only 0.0001 of carrying capacity.
