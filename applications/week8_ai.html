Module 8: AI Application — Attention and Convex Geometry

The weighted averages from Module 3 reappear as points inside a convex region.

A question for this module
What shape is hidden inside an attention-weighted average?

Back in Module 3, attention was introduced as a weighted combination. Now you have the geometric language to say something stronger about that calculation.

Start with familiar mathematics

The Module 8 idea

A convex combination of two points \(A\) and \(B\) has the form \[ P=wA+(1-w)B, \qquad 0\le w\le1. \] Every such point lies on the line segment joining \(A\) and \(B\). With more points, convex combinations fill their convex hull.
Now put that mathematics to work

The AI connection

Attention weights are nonnegative and, after normalization, add to 1. That means an attention-weighted combination of value vectors is a convex combination. To make this visible, imagine two token representations in a two-dimensional plane: \[ A=(2,1),\qquad B=(8,5). \] Real representations have many more coordinates; two dimensions let us see the geometry.
Keep the scale in perspective. These numbers are deliberately small so the calculation is visible. A real LLM uses a much larger vocabulary, high-dimensional vectors, and many repeated layers. We are isolating one genuine mathematical structure at a time.
Follow one example

Work through the calculation

Use weights 0.75 and 0.25: \[ P=0.75A+0.25B. \] Compute each coordinate: \[ x=0.75(2)+0.25(8)=3.5, \] \[ y=0.75(1)+0.25(5)=2. \] Thus \[ P=(3.5,2). \] Because the weights are nonnegative and add to 1, \(P\) lies on the segment from \(A\) to \(B\). So the weighted-average idea from statistics has become a geometric statement.

Where this leaves us
This is a good example of one mathematical object wearing two names: \[ \text{weighted average in statistics} \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad \text{convex combination in geometry}. \] The language changes because our viewpoint changes. The underlying calculation does not.
