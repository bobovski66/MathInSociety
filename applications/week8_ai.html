Module 8: AI Application — Attention and Convex Geometry

Why weighted combinations naturally live inside a feasible geometric region.

Why this page exists

Mathematics gives us a way into AI

In Module 3 we treated attention as a weighted average. Now we can see the geometry hiding inside that idea. If nonnegative weights add to 1, the weighted combination is a convex combination.

Important: the examples on this page are intentionally tiny models. Real LLMs use huge vectors, many layers, and billions of learned numerical parameters. We simplify the scale while keeping the mathematical structure recognizable.

Connection to this module

The mathematics you already know

Convex geometry gives a visual interpretation of attention: combining points with weights that are nonnegative and sum to 1 keeps the result inside their convex hull.

AI vocabulary

Four ideas to carry with you

embeddingA point or vector used to represent a token numerically.
convex combinationA weighted sum with nonnegative weights that total 1.
convex hullThe smallest convex region containing a collection of points.
attention outputA weighted combination of value vectors in a transformer attention calculation.
Worked example

Follow the numbers

Take two simplified token embeddings \[ A=(2,1),\qquad B=(8,5). \] Using weights 0.75 and 0.25 gives \[ 0.75A+0.25B =(0.75\cdot2+0.25\cdot8,;0.75\cdot1+0.25\cdot5) =(3.5,2). \] This point lies on the line segment connecting \(A\) and \(B\).
Investigation

Try the mathematics yourself

1. Do weights 0.75 and 0.25 define a convex combination?

2. For A=(2,1) and B=(8,5), find the x-coordinate of 0.75A+0.25B.

3. Find the y-coordinate.

4. Where must every convex combination of A and B lie?

What to remember

AI idea of the week

This is a surprisingly deep bridge: attention uses probability-like weights, and probability-like weights create convex combinations. Statistical weighting and convex geometry are two views of the same structure.

You do not need to know every engineering detail of an LLM to reason mathematically about what it is doing. We will keep adding one mathematical layer at a time.
