How a route recommender uses a network

A route planner offers two ways to reach a hospital. Which one would you choose, and why?

What a route planner knows: Navigation software records places and the direct routes between them. It can look for the fewest connections or the least travel time, depending on what matters for the trip.

A hospital route with two choices

A patient walks from a station S to hospital H. Route 1 goes S–C–H and has two walkways: 8 minutes and 9 minutes. Route 2 goes S–L–P–H and has three walkways: 4 minutes, 3 minutes, and 5 minutes.

Fewest links: S–C–H (2 walkways).

Fastest according to the stated times: S–L–P–H (12 minutes, compared with 17).

What matters for this trip?

0 minutes

How does a computer decide?

For an unweighted network, a simple breadth-first search examines locations one edge away, then two edges away, and so on. For a network with nonnegative travel times, a shortest-time algorithm such as Dijkstra's algorithm compares accumulated minutes instead. Both work from stated data and rules; the diagram itself does not tell the program which objective the user values.

Questions worth asking

  1. Why would someone prefer fewer transfers even if the trip takes longer?

  3. Which information might be missing from a route recommender for a wheelchair user?
