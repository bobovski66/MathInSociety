Week 4 Application — Checking a Nutrition Label

Use a sample mean and margin of error to decide whether a company's claimed population mean is consistent with the observed data.

How to complete this application

  1. Construct the interval before comparing it with the label claim.
  2. Keep the conclusion at the population-mean level; the interval is not a statement about every individual box.
  3. Then use the square-root sample-size rule to see what changes when the sample is quadrupled.

Math model

An estimate with margin \(E\) gives \([\hat\theta-E,\hat\theta+E]\). For similar studies, \(E\propto1/\sqrt n\), so quadrupling \(n\) approximately halves the margin of error.

Context

The claim and the sample

A cereal label states that one serving contains an average of 230 calories. A consumer group cannot test every box produced, so it randomly selects 64 boxes. The sample has a mean of 243 calories per serving. The reported margin of error is ±8 calories.

Population

All boxes of this cereal produced under the process of interest.

The label's claim, 230 calories, is a claim about the population mean.

Sample

The 64 tested boxes.

The observed mean, 243 calories, is a sample statistic used to estimate the population mean.

Model used here: confidence interval = sample estimate − margin of error to sample estimate + margin of error.

Part 2

Make the statistical decision

Notice that the confidence interval is about the population mean. It does not tell us the exact calorie count of every individual box.

Part 3 · Precision

What if the sample were larger?

For similar data, margin of error is approximately proportional to 1/√n. If the consumer group quadruples the sample size from 64 boxes to 256 boxes, the margin of error is approximately cut in half.

Result

What the sample says

The original interval is 235 to 251 calories, so 230 is outside it. Under the simplified sample-size relationship used in the chapter, quadrupling the sample would give an approximate margin of error of 4 calories and an interval of 239 to 247 calories. The larger sample gives a narrower estimate, but the label claim is still outside the interval.
