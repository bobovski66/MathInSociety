Step 1 · Module 5 mathematics

Rare events carry more information

In Module 5, information content is measured by \[ I=-\log_2(p). \] When \(p\) is small, the event is surprising and \(I\) is large. For probabilities that are powers of 2, the calculation is especially simple: \[ p=\frac18=2^{-3}\quad\Rightarrow\quad I=3\text{ bits}. \] An event with probability \(1/64=2^{-6}\) carries 6 bits.
Step 2 · Make the AI connection

A language model can be scored by the surprise of the correct token

Suppose the actual next token in some training text is “river.” Before seeing it, a model assigns “river” a probability. If the model gives it high probability, the observed token is not very surprising. If the model gives it tiny probability, the observed token is very surprising. This gives us a numerical way to judge predictions. We look at the probability the model assigned to what actually happened, then convert that probability into bits of surprise.

Keep the scale in perspective: this page uses tiny numbers so you can see the mathematics. Real language models use many-dimensional vectors, very large vocabularies, and many repeated layers. The simplified calculation is a model of one mathematical structure, not a miniature copy of the whole system.

Step 3 · Worked example

Compare two predictions of the same observed token

Imagine the correct next token is “river.” Model A assigns \[ P(\text{river})=\frac12, \] so \[ I_A=-\log_2(1/2)=1\text{ bit}. \] Model B assigns \[ P(\text{river})=\frac1{32}, \] so \[ I_B=-\log_2(1/32)=5\text{ bits}. \] The observed token was much less surprising to Model A. Across many tokens, we can average these surprise values. Training methods try to make the actual text less surprising on average.
New vocabulary

Name the ideas after seeing the mathematics

These terms describe structures you have already used above.

surprisalThe information value \(-\log_2 p\) of an observed outcome.
bitA unit of information.
lossA numerical score that training tries to reduce; next-token surprise is closely related to a standard language-model loss.

What to carry forward

The AI idea of this module

Module 5 reveals why probability and information theory sit at the heart of language models: \[ \text{good prediction}\longleftrightarrow\text{low surprise}\longleftrightarrow\text{fewer bits}. \] That is also why prediction and compression are mathematically close relatives.
