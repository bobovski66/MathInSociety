Step 1 · Module 5 mathematics

Probability becomes information

Module 5 introduced

\[I=-\log_2(p).\]

For powers of 2, the calculation is especially simple:

ProbabilityInformation
\(1/2\)1 bit
\(1/4\)2 bits
\(1/8\)3 bits
\(1/32\)5 bits

Rare events carry more information because they are more surprising.

Step 2 · Make the AI connection

Apply the same formula to the token that actually occurred

Suppose the correct next token in a training example is “mat.” Model A gives “mat” probability \(1/2\), while Model B gives it probability \(1/32\).

For Model A,

\[I_A=-\log_2(1/2)=1\text{ bit}.\]

For Model B,

\[I_B=-\log_2(1/32)=5\text{ bits}.\]

The actual token was much less surprising to Model A. In this narrow sense, Model A predicted this example better.

Step 3 · Worked example

Average the surprise over several tokens

Suppose four observed tokens produce surprise values

\[1,3,2,2\text{ bits}.\]

The average is

\[\frac{1+3+2+2}{4}=2\text{ bits per token}.\]

Machine-learning texts often call an average of this kind cross-entropy. The name is new; the arithmetic is an ordinary mean of information values. This also connects to compression: predictable symbols require fewer bits on average.

A few new words

Vocabulary after the mathematics

These terms name ideas you have already seen above.

surprisalAnother name for \(-\log_2p\), the information of an observed outcome.
cross-entropyAn average surprise of observed data under a model.
lossA numerical quantity training tries to reduce.
compressionRepresenting predictable information using fewer bits on average.

What to carry forward

The AI idea of this module

Module 5 gives a quantitative training idea:

\[\text{better prediction}\Longrightarrow\text{lower average surprise}.\]

The same mathematics connects probability, information, model training, and data compression.

These examples are intentionally small. Real LLMs use much larger numerical objects and many repeated layers, but the mathematical structure highlighted here is genuine.
