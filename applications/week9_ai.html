Module 9: AI Application — Allocating Limited AI Compute

A linear program can model competing uses of limited computational resources.

A question for this module
If two AI tasks compete for the same limited resources, how could we decide how much of each to run?

Optimization begins only after a goal and some constraints have been chosen. That distinction matters in AI because computers can optimize a mathematical objective very efficiently without deciding whether the objective itself was a wise one.

Start with familiar mathematics

The Module 9 idea

A linear program needs three ingredients: decision variables, linear constraints, and an objective function. The constraints create a feasible region. For a bounded polygonal feasible region, a linear objective reaches an extreme value at a vertex.
Now put that mathematics to work

The AI connection

Suppose an organization runs two kinds of AI jobs. Let \[ x=\text{hundreds of text jobs per day}, \] and \[ y=\text{hundreds of analysis jobs per day}. \] Text jobs and analysis jobs compete for computing capacity and staff-review time. We will model those limits linearly. The numbers below are hypothetical. More importantly, the “benefit score” is also a human-chosen model—not a fact discovered by the mathematics.
Keep the scale in perspective. These numbers are deliberately small so the calculation is visible. A real LLM uses a much larger vocabulary, high-dimensional vectors, and many repeated layers. We are isolating one genuine mathematical structure at a time.
Follow one example

Work through the calculation

Suppose the resource limits are \[ x+2y\le10 \] for computing capacity, and \[ 2x+y\le14 \] for staff review, with \(x,y\ge0\). Suppose the chosen objective is \[ B=3x+5y. \] Test the point \((2,3)\): \[ 2+2(3)=8\le10, \] and \[ 2(2)+3=7\le14. \] So \((2,3)\) is feasible. Its objective value is \[ B=3(2)+5(3)=21. \] We would compare that value with the objective at the other vertices.

Where this leaves us
Linear programming can answer, “Which feasible point best fits this objective?” It cannot answer, “Was this the right objective for society to choose?” That second question belongs to the people who built the model.
