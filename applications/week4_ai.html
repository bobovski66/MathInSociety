AI strand so far: So far we have described pieces of a model. Module 4 changes the question: how do we measure how well a model performs?
Step 1 · Module 4 mathematics

A sample tells us about a larger group

Suppose we want the proportion of questions in a large question bank that a system can answer correctly. If 171 of 200 sampled questions are correct, then

\[\hat p=\frac{171}{200}=0.855=85.5\%.\]

This sample statistic describes the 200 tested questions exactly and can be used to estimate performance on a larger population of questions.

Step 2 · Make the AI connection

An AI benchmark is a statistical measurement

A benchmark is a collection of tasks used to test a model. For our classroom example, imagine 200 questions are randomly selected from a clearly defined bank of 10,000 questions.

If the model gets 85.5% correct, that does not mean “the AI is 85.5% intelligent.” It means the model answered 85.5% of this sample correctly.

Real-world caution: many real benchmarks are fixed collections rather than random samples. A confidence interval can describe sampling uncertainty only when the sampling model makes sense. It cannot make an unrepresentative benchmark representative.

Step 3 · Worked example

Attach uncertainty to the estimate

Suppose the sample accuracy is 85.5% and a Module 4 calculation gives a margin of error of 5 percentage points. Then

\[85.5\%\pm5\%= [80.5\%,90.5\%].\]

Under our classroom sampling assumptions, this estimates performance on the defined population of questions. It does not estimate every possible thing an AI might ever be asked to do.

A few new words

Vocabulary after the mathematics

These terms name ideas you have already seen above.

benchmarkA set of tasks used to evaluate a model.
sample accuracyThe proportion of tested tasks answered correctly.
populationThe larger set of tasks we want the sample to represent.
representative sampleA sample selected in a way that supports inference to the intended population.

What to carry forward

The AI idea of this module

AI performance numbers are statistical claims. Module 4 gives you the right questions: What was sampled? What population is it supposed to represent? How uncertain is the estimate? Was the sampling method reasonable?

These examples are intentionally small. Real LLMs use much larger numerical objects and many repeated layers, but the mathematical structure highlighted here is genuine.
