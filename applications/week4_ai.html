Step 1 · Module 4 mathematics

A sample statistic is not the whole population

In Module 4 you use a sample to estimate something about a larger population. Suppose 82 of 100 sampled items have a certain property. The sample proportion is \[ \hat p=\frac{82}{100}=0.82. \] A confidence interval or margin of error reminds us that a sample result is an estimate, not perfect knowledge of the whole population.
Step 2 · Make the AI connection

An AI benchmark is also a sample

When you hear that an AI model scored “82%” on a benchmark, that score came from a particular set of test questions. The questions are the sample. The broader collection of tasks we hope the test represents is the population of interest. This matters because different benchmarks contain different kinds of questions. A model can perform well on one collection and less well on another. The arithmetic of a percentage is easy; the statistical question is whether the sample represents what we care about.

Keep the scale in perspective: this page uses tiny numbers so you can see the mathematics. Real language models use many-dimensional vectors, very large vocabularies, and many repeated layers. The simplified calculation is a model of one mathematical structure, not a miniature copy of the whole system.

Step 3 · Worked example

Build and interpret a simple interval

Suppose a model answers 82 of 100 benchmark questions correctly: \[ \hat p=0.82=82\%. \] For practice, suppose we are told to use a margin of error of 8 percentage points. Then \[ 82\%\pm8\%=[74\%,90\%]. \] This interval does not mean “the AI is between 74% and 90% intelligent.” It estimates performance for a particular population of tasks under the assumptions of the sampling procedure.
New vocabulary

These terms describe structures you have already used above.

benchmarkA collection of test tasks used to measure a model.
sample accuracyThe fraction of tested items answered correctly.
population of tasksThe broader class of questions we want the benchmark to represent.

The AI idea of this module
