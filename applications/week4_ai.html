Module 4: AI Application — Measuring an AI System with Samples

Benchmark scores make more sense when we treat them as statistics.

A question for this module
What does it actually mean when someone says an AI system scored 85%?

A percentage sounds precise, but a score is only meaningful after we know what was measured. AI systems are often tested on collections of questions called benchmarks. That puts us directly in the world of samples, proportions, and inference.

Start with familiar mathematics

The Module 4 idea

Suppose 82 out of 100 sampled items have a property. The sample proportion is \[ \hat p=\frac{82}{100}=0.82. \] The sample tells us something about a larger population, but it does not magically become the whole population. That is why sampling method, sample size, and uncertainty matter.
Now put that mathematics to work

The AI connection

Suppose an AI model answers 82 of 100 benchmark questions correctly. Its sample accuracy is 82%. The 100 benchmark questions are the sample. The population we care about might be “questions of this general type,” not “every question anyone could ever ask.” A benchmark result is therefore partly a statement about the model and partly a statement about the benchmark.
Keep the scale in perspective. These numbers are deliberately small so the calculation is visible. A real LLM uses a much larger vocabulary, high-dimensional vectors, and many repeated layers. We are isolating one genuine mathematical structure at a time.
Follow one example

Work through the calculation

For practice, suppose an 82% benchmark score is reported with a margin of error of 8 percentage points. Then \[ 82\%\pm8\%=[74\%,90\%]. \] The interval is about performance on a defined population of tasks under a statistical model. It is not an “intelligence interval,” and it does not say that every future answer has a probability between 74% and 90% of being correct.

Where this leaves us
The useful habit here is statistical rather than technological. When you see an AI score, ask: What was sampled? What counted as correct? What larger set of tasks is this score supposed to represent? Those questions turn a headline number back into mathematics.
