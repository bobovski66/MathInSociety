Step 1 · Module 7 mathematics

Separate fixed cost from variable cost

In Module 7, a common cost model is \[ C(q)=F+vq, \] where \(F\) is fixed cost and \(v\) is variable cost per unit. Revenue might be \[ R(q)=pq, \] where \(p\) is the price per unit. Profit is \[ P(q)=R(q)-C(q). \] Break-even occurs where \(R(q)=C(q)\).
Step 2 · Make the AI connection

An AI service also has fixed and per-use costs

A company operating an AI tutoring service might pay for servers, software, engineering, and other monthly expenses before a single student uses the service. Those are represented by a simplified fixed cost. Each additional session also uses computing resources; we represent that by a variable cost per session. The numbers below are hypothetical. The point is to use the Module 7 model on a modern context.

Keep the scale in perspective: this page uses tiny numbers so you can see the mathematics. Real language models use many-dimensional vectors, very large vocabularies, and many repeated layers. The simplified calculation is a model of one mathematical structure, not a miniature copy of the whole system.

Step 3 · Worked example

Build the functions before solving

Suppose monthly fixed cost is $6,000, variable cost is $0.40 per tutoring session, and revenue is $2.40 per session. \[ C(q)=6000+0.40q, \] \[ R(q)=2.40q. \] Therefore \[ P(q)=2.40q-(6000+0.40q)=2q-6000. \] For break-even, \[ 2q-6000=0, \] so \[ q=3000. \] In this simplified model, 3,000 sessions are needed before monthly revenue equals monthly cost.
