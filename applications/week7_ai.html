Module 7: AI Application — What Does an AI Service Cost?

Cost, revenue, profit, and break-even mathematics apply to AI services too.

A question for this module
An AI response looks almost free on a screen—but what would a cost model say?

Digital services can hide their physical costs. Servers, electricity, engineering, and network infrastructure are real resources. Module 7 gives us a simple way to turn those resources into a model we can inspect.

Start with familiar mathematics

The Module 7 idea

A common cost model is \[ C(q)=F+vq, \] where \(F\) is fixed cost and \(v\) is variable cost per unit. If revenue is \[ R(q)=pq, \] then profit is \[ P(q)=R(q)-C(q). \] Break-even occurs when revenue and cost are equal.
Now put that mathematics to work

The AI connection

Suppose a small AI tutoring service has $6,000 per month in fixed costs. Each student session uses computing resources costing an average of $0.40. The service receives $2.40 per session. These numbers are hypothetical. They are not meant to estimate any particular company's costs. They simply let us use the same cost model on an AI setting.
Keep the scale in perspective. These numbers are deliberately small so the calculation is visible. A real LLM uses a much larger vocabulary, high-dimensional vectors, and many repeated layers. We are isolating one genuine mathematical structure at a time.
Follow one example

Work through the calculation

The monthly cost function is \[ C(q)=6000+0.40q. \] Revenue is \[ R(q)=2.40q. \] Therefore \[ P(q)=2.40q-(6000+0.40q)=2q-6000. \] Set profit equal to zero: \[ 2q-6000=0. \] So \[ q=3000. \] In this simplified model, the service breaks even at 3,000 sessions per month.

Where this leaves us
The point of a mathematical model is not that its assumptions are automatically true. The point is that the assumptions are visible. If electricity prices, staffing, or revenue change, we can change the numbers and see exactly how the conclusion changes.
