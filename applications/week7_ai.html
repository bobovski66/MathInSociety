Module 7: AI Application — What Does an AI Service Cost?

Fixed cost, per-use cost, revenue, profit, and break-even.

Why this page exists

Mathematics gives us a way into AI

An AI product has a mathematical business structure just like many other services. There may be a large fixed cost to develop and operate the system, plus a smaller variable cost each time someone uses it.

Important: the examples on this page are intentionally tiny models. Real LLMs use huge vectors, many layers, and billions of learned numerical parameters. We simplify the scale while keeping the mathematical structure recognizable.

Connection to this module

The mathematics you already know

Module 7 lets us model this directly with cost, revenue, profit, and break-even functions.

AI vocabulary

Four ideas to carry with you

fixed costA cost that does not depend on the number of uses in the model.
variable costAn additional cost per unit of activity.
inferenceThe computation performed when a trained model responds to a prompt.
break-evenThe activity level where revenue equals cost.
Worked example

Follow the numbers

Suppose a small AI tutoring service has monthly fixed costs of $6,000 and an average computing cost of $0.40 per student session. It earns $2.40 per session. \[ C(q)=6000+0.40q,\qquad R(q)=2.40q. \] Profit is \[ P(q)=R(q)-C(q)=2q-6000. \] Break-even occurs when \(P(q)=0\), so \(q=3000\) sessions.
Investigation

Try the mathematics yourself

1. For C(q)=6000+0.40q, what is the cost of 1000 sessions?

2. For R(q)=2.40q, what is the revenue from 1000 sessions?

3. How many sessions are needed to break even?

4. What is the marginal profit per additional session after the simplified fixed cost is covered?

What to remember

AI idea of the week

AI may look immaterial, but every response uses infrastructure, electricity, hardware, and labor. Cost models turn vague claims about “cheap AI” or “expensive AI” into quantities we can inspect.

You do not need to know every engineering detail of an LLM to reason mathematically about what it is doing. We will keep adding one mathematical layer at a time.
