\section*{Chapter Review}
The problems below bring together the three main investigations. Explain your working rather than providing only the final number.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Follow the Collatz rule from 10 until first reaching 1. Report the number of updates and the largest value visited.
\item Compare Collatz starts 6 and 7. Why is a collection of successfully tested starting values not a proof that all positive integers reach 1?
\item A program checks every binary string of length 10. How many strings must it inspect? A balanced search can distinguish that many options in how many binary decisions?
\item For $x_{n+1}=2.8x_n(1-x_n)$ with $x_0=0.2$, calculate $x_1$ and $x_2$. Why must the second update use the new value?
\item Describe the behavior of logistic-map trajectories for $r=2.8$, $r=3.2$, and $r=3.9$ from the same initial state. Which setting demonstrates sensitive dependence?
\item Two logistic-map starts are $0.4000$ and $0.4001$. Find the gap between the initial states. Explain how a fixed rule can eventually produce separated forecasts without introducing randomness.
\item Construct stages 0 through 4 of the Sierpi\'nski triangle. How many small triangles remain at stage 4? What fraction of the original filled area remains?
\item A stage-3 Sierpi\'nski triangle has 27 small triangles. How long is each small side relative to the original? Why does the total area decrease even though the number of triangles grows?
\item (Extension.) Three half-size copies reproduce the Sierpi\'nski triangle. Solve $2^D=3$ for its similarity dimension $D$.
\item Write a short comparison showing why computational complexity, chaotic sensitivity, and self-similar geometry are distinct properties. Name one computation or observation that supports each.
\end{enumerate}
