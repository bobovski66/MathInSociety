Module 9 · Laboratory

Make the choices. Find the pattern.

A community center uses two symbols to record requests. First count with them; then use them to find routes and give instructions.

Begin with the first example. Each section shows one calculation or route before asking you to try the next. Use the explorer beside this page when you want to check a tree. Your answers save automatically.

1. Binary numbers

The place values from left to right are 16, 8, 4, 2, 1. Use a 1 if you include that value, and a 0 if you skip it.

Example. 10101 means 16 + 4 + 1 = 21. To write ten in binary, use 8 + 2: 01010, normally written 1010.

2. Following tree addresses

Each junction asks you to go left (0) or right (1). The digits of an address record the choices in order.

Example. The address 10 means right, then left. Another choice extends that address to 100 or 101.

3. A small decision program

Now use the branches as instructions. A caller enters 0 or 1 and stops when a service appears.

Example. Enter 1 and then 0. Code 10 reaches bus times after two questions. The caller stops there.

4. Count the questions

Today eight callers need pantry hours, four need bus times, two need regular rides and two need urgent rides.

Example. Eight pantry callers answer one question each: 8 × 1 = 8 questions. Four bus callers answer two each: 4 × 2 = 8 more.

5. One rule removes some routes

Suppose a binary address may never contain two 1s together. Of the two-digit codes, 00, 01 and 10 are allowed, but 11 is forbidden.

Example. The five legal three-digit addresses are 000, 001, 010, 100, 101. That is five instead of eight.

Use the idea in a place you know

Think of a service menu, questionnaire, device, or task that asks someone to make choices. Describe two possible choices and where they lead. What important detail could such a simple tree leave out?
