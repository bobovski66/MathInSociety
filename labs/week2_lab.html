Week 2 Laboratory — From Counts to Probability

Use samples, frequency tables, relative frequencies, graphs, and probability distributions to summarize observed data.

Before you begin

  1. Identify the population and sample before using any counts.
  2. Build frequencies first, then divide by the sample size to obtain relative frequencies.
  3. Choose a graph from the type of variable: categories use bars; quantitative intervals use a histogram.

Key notation

For category \(i\), relative frequency is \(f_i/n\). A valid probability distribution has \(p_i\ge0\) and \(\sum p_i=1\). Also, \(P(\text{not }A)=1-P(A)\).

Part 1 · Sampling

Population, sample, and representativeness

Course viewpoint: every sample is only a partial view of its population, so a realized sample will differ from the population in some ways. Rather than sort samples into “biased” and “unbiased,” focus here on how the sampling method may overrepresent or underrepresent parts of the population.

Hospital wait times

Ballot poll
