Investigating connections

A guided lab: count, trace, test a failure, propose a repair, and explain the result.

This lab has five short investigations. Use the network explorer when a question asks you to change a connection. Each answer saves in this browser as you type. Print or save your completed lab when finished.

1. Count direct partnerships

A food pantry P, library L, school S, community fridge F, and senior hub H have direct partnerships PL, PS, LS, SF, FH.

2. Follow a route

Use the county network from the reading, with edges AB, AC, BC, BD, CD, DE, EF, EG, FG.

Open the explorer in a second tab

3. Identify a vulnerable connection

In the explorer, choose no new link and switch on the D–E failure.

Open the explorer in a second tab

4. Spend the budget on one improvement

Leave D–E failed. Compare adding A–D with adding D–F.

Open the explorer in a second tab

5. Follow an alert through time

Restore the original network. Set A as the start and advance the alert one round at a time.

Final reflection: from model to reality

Choose a real network you know: a bus system, a food-sharing group, a workplace, a friendship group, or something else. Describe its nodes and edges. What is one question that a simple graph could help answer, and what is one important detail that such a graph would miss?
