Week 10 Laboratory — Recursion, Fractals, and Prediction

Compare stable and sensitive recursions, compute Collatz trajectories, measure fractal growth, iterate the logistic map, and examine scale-dependent measurement.

Before you begin

  1. Write the recursion rule and starting value before iterating.
  2. For a trajectory, keep track of both the number of steps and the largest value reached.
  3. When measurement changes with scale, distinguish a change in the measured number from a change in the physical object.

Key notation

Collatz: even \(n\mapsto n/2\), odd \(n\mapsto3n+1\). Koch length multiplies by \(4/3\) per step; Sierpinski area multiplies by \(3/4\). Logistic map: \(x_{n+1}=rx_n(1-x_n)\).

Three different phenomena

Some recursive rules keep nearby starting values close. Some grow differences predictably. Chaotic rules can amplify very small initial differences until long-range predictions disagree substantially.
