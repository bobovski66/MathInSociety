Center and spread

Use the mean, median, mode, range, and sample standard deviation to explain what a set of observations does—and does not—tell us.

About this investigation

Two groups can have the same average and still look very different. One might cluster closely around its typical value, while another varies widely. To describe a data set carefully, we need measures of both center and spread.

In this lab you will use ordinary situations—complaints, scores, deliveries, and unusual observations—to decide what different summaries reveal. Carry totals and intermediate results forward when requested. For sample standard deviation, divide by n−1 and round the final SD to two decimals.

For sample standard deviation, use n − 1. Round SD answers to two decimal places. Answers save as you work.

Part 2. Totals, missing values, and range

Part 3. Build a sample-SD table

Standard deviation measures a typical scale of departure from the mean. Begin with the mean of 3, 5, 5, and 7, then find each observation's signed deviation. The positive and negative deviations cancel, so square each deviation before adding.

With four observations, the sample variance is the sum of squared deviations divided by 3. The sample standard deviation is the square root of that variance. Use the table step by step so every quantity has a clear role.
