Part 1 / Constant dollar additions

Simple interest and missing information

Worked example · A different starting amount

A $600 account at 5% simple annual interest adds $30 each year. Four years adds $120, so the total is $720. To find an unknown rate, divide the interest earned by principal times years. To find time, divide interest earned by the fixed yearly interest.

Part 2 / Repeated multiplication

Count the factors before using the calculator

Annual compounding uses one factor per year. Monthly compounding uses rate r/12 and twelve times as many factors. For example, $400 at 10% annually becomes $440 and then $484 over two years. A fixed $40 addition each year would instead give $480.

Now compare three models for the same $1,500 at 6% for ten years. Hold the starting amount, nominal annual rate, and duration fixed.

Part 3 / Rate per step and steps per year

Compounding frequency and effective yield

Part 4 / Estimate, then interpret

Doubling and two doublings

Use the Rule of 72: divide 72 by the annual percentage number. These questions assume fixed percentage growth continues without withdrawals or payments. The answers are estimates in years, except the field asking for a rate.

Part 5 / Carry the ending balance forward

A $3,000 loan with $150 monthly payments

The nominal annual rate is 9%, divided across twelve monthly updates. Thus \(i=0.09/12=0.0075\), or 0.75% monthly. Interest is added first, then $150 is paid. There are no additional charges.

Round each month’s interest to cents, subtract the payment, and carry the resulting cent balance into the next row. The provided starting balances help you check continuity; they are not all $3,000.
