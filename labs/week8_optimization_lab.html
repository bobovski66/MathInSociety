Week 8 / Optimization Extension

Build a model. Defend its answer.

Solve complete maximum and minimum problems, compare competing objectives, and explain when several plans are equally good.

Five complete investigationsMaximum & minimumCompeting goals & ties

From a situation to a justified recommendation

The core lab introduced the geometry. Here you will write and solve complete linear programs, compare different goals, and recognize multiple best choices. Use the optimizer as a check after you have written the model and predicted a result.

All quantities and prices are invented. Assume resource use and per-unit contribution remain constant over the stated range. Acres and other divisible quantities may be fractional; for indivisible products, check whether a continuous answer is usable.

Keep the six steps in order

  1. Define x and y, with units.
  2. Write every constraint, including nonnegativity.
  3. Write the objective and its direction.
  4. Find and verify all feasible corners.
  5. Compare the objective values; inspect ties and unbounded directions.
  6. Report a feasible plan, its score, and the relevant assumptions.

Part 1 / Translate a resource table

Land and labor for two crops

Let x be acres of corn and y acres of soybeans for one season. Corn uses 3 labor-hours per acre and contributes $200 per acre after per-acre costs. Soybeans use 2 labor-hours per acre and contribute $150 per acre. At most 120 labor-hours and 50 acres are available. The objective is to maximize total contribution.

For example, 10 acres of corn require 3(10)=30 labor-hours. Sum the use of both crops before comparing it with the available amount. Include x≥0 and y≥0 as well as the two resource limits.

Part 2 / A complete maximum

Best contribution from two resource limits

\[\text{Maximize }f=5x+8y\quad\text{subject to }x+2y\leq16,\quad3x+2y\leq24,\quad x,y\geq0.\]

Think of x and y as two production quantities, with two resources and contributions $5 and $8 per unit. The boundary equations have the same 2y term, so subtract them to find x, then substitute to find y. The listed corners must also be checked against both inequalities.

Part 3 / Requirements instead of allowances

The cheapest plan may be an axis corner

A fictional supply order offers bundle A with 1 folder and 2 cards for $3, or bundle B with 1 folder and 1 card for $5. We need at least 8 folders and 10 cards. With x A-bundles and y B-bundles, the model is

\[\text{Minimize }C=3x+5y,\qquad x+y\geq8,\quad2x+y\geq10,\quad x,y\geq0.\]

Find the lower boundary corners. The region extends upward and rightward, but positive prices mean buying indefinitely more cannot reduce cost forever. A mixed plan is not automatically cheapest; compare all three relevant vertices.

Part 4 / Keep the allowed set fixed

Three questions about the same four corners

A bounded polygon has vertices (0,0), (8,0), (5,4), and (0,6). Compare the contribution score π=10x+12y and revenue R=15x+8y at all corners. Also compare the cost C=4x+6y for the three listed nonzero corner plans.

The cost comparison has a deliberately limited scope

Choosing the cheapest among three listed plans is not the same as minimizing cost over the whole polygon. Over the full region, (0,0) costs zero. Simply deleting the origin does not create a sensible production requirement: a tiny positive plan can have a cost below all three listed corners. To require production, add an explicit lower-bound constraint and find the new feasible region.

Part 5 / An optimal edge

Two best corners can mean infinitely many continuous solutions

Use the filled polygon with vertices (0,0), (4,0), (3,2), and (0,5), in boundary order. Maximize f=3x+3y. The equal coefficients mean each unit of either quantity adds 3, so this goal rewards the total x+y.

Before you submit

Check that each answer includes the right kind of quantity: a coordinate, a plan, a total, or a number of vertices. A cost is not a location; a single vertex is not the entire optimal set when an edge ties. Written responses should explain what the computed number means.
