Week 9 Laboratory — Linear Programming

Build linear programs, solve maximization and minimization problems, and see how changing the objective can change the optimal corner.

Before you begin

  1. Define the decision variables in words and units before writing any inequality.
  2. Translate each limitation or requirement into a constraint, then write the objective separately.
  3. Evaluate the objective at every feasible vertex and state the final answer in the units of the original problem.

Key notation

A linear program has decision variables, constraints, and an objective \(f=ax+by\). For the bounded two-variable problems in this course, a maximum or minimum occurs at a feasible vertex; a tie at adjacent vertices makes the entire joining edge optimal.

Part 1 · Build the model

Farmer: corn and soybeans

Let x be acres of corn and y be acres of soybeans. Corn uses 3 labor-hours per acre and earns $200 profit. Soybeans use 2 labor-hours per acre and earn $150 profit. The farmer has at most 120 labor-hours and 50 acres of land.

Part 3 · Minimization

Requirements point the other way

Minimize C = 3x + 5y
subject to x + y ≥ 8, 2x + y ≥ 10, x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0.

Because these are minimum requirements, the feasible region extends up and to the right. The minimum is still found at a boundary vertex.

Relevant vertex(8,0)(2,6)(0,10)
C=3x+5y
