Week 1 Laboratory — How Numbers Behave

Numbers are not only labels. They can locate, move, scale, compare, and generate new values. This lab follows those roles from the number tower through recursion.

Before you begin

  1. Classify or compute first; use the representation named in the section rather than jumping to a calculator.
  2. When a situation describes direction, encode direction with signed numbers before doing the arithmetic.
  3. For recursion, write the update rule and calculate the first few steps before comparing long-run behavior.

Key notation

The number systems satisfy \(\mathbb N\subset\mathbb Z\subset\mathbb Q\subset\mathbb R\). Fixed addition has \(a_{n+1}=a_n+d\); fixed-factor growth has \(b_{n+1}=rb_n\).

Laboratory question

What changes when we change our way of looking at a number?

The goal is not to race through arithmetic. In each part, notice the representation that makes the structure easiest to see: containment, motion, scaling, equivalence, or repeated change.

Part 1 · Number tower

Classify by the smallest system that works

For each value, choose the smallest set containing it: natural (N), integer (Z), rational (Q), or real but irrational (R).

Choose the smallest set because it tells you more about the number than the broader label “real.”

Part 2 · Additive view

Turn arithmetic into motion

A diver begins at the surface, descends 15 m, rises 8 m, then descends 5 m. Treat upward motion as positive and downward motion as negative.

start: 0after descent: −15after ascent: −7final: −12

Once the signs are chosen consistently, the arithmetic is just tracking position.

With the sign convention fixed, downward motion is a negative addend and upward motion is a positive addend.
