Cybernetics: the science behind the sequences

A cybernetic system uses information about its changing state to guide later action. In this greenhouse: the state is temperature, the sensor is the thermometer, the reference is the target temperature, the controller decides on a correction, and the actuator changes the heat. The next measurement closes the loop.

Corrective (negative) feedback acts against a departure. Amplifying (positive) feedback reinforces a change. Adaptive feedback changes later behavior after measuring past results. The controls below let you explore what happens when the report reaches the controller late.

Connect to Week 4: when measurements vary

In the first graph, the controller is given an exact measurement of the state (or a delayed but otherwise exact one). A real thermometer does not always report the same reading, even when the temperature is steady. Think back to the normal distribution and its standard deviation: a larger standard deviation means more spread in possible readings.

Suppose a greenhouse is really at 68°F and individual sensor readings are approximately normal, with mean 68°F and standard deviation 2°F. A reading of 72°F has z-score (72 − 68) ÷ 2 = 2. That is unusual, but possible without a real change in temperature.

Think about it: Averages from more measurements tend to vary less. But if you need to wait to collect them, the decision arrives later. How could delay change the outcome?
