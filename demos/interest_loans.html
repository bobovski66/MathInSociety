Five investigations

  1. For savings at $2,000 and 3%, compare years 1, 5, and 30 with annual compounding. Why do the first-year amounts tie?
  2. Set principal $1,000, annual rate 6%, and ten years. Change only compounding frequency. Compare the effective yield with the quoted nominal rate.
  3. For the reading’s loan, compare month 1 with month 5. Which part of the $200 payment changes, and why?
  4. Choose interest exactly covered, then payment below interest. Explain the distinction between a constant debt and a growing debt. In each case, what does the payment cover?
  5. Return to the reading’s loan and limit the simulation to six months. Explain why six is not its payoff month. Then restore a long enough horizon and compare the two rounding methods.

For the application’s original comparison results, select its presets and keep full internal precision. For the reading, lab’s hand table, and discussion, use monthly cent rounding. The difference is part of the model and is stated with each result.
