Choose “Twice the original counts.” What doubles? What stays the same? Then try “Only A” and “Empty sample.”

Compare your reasoning

All counts and the total double, so each share stays the same. “Only A” gives A a relative frequency of 1. With no observations, shares are undefined because their denominator would be zero.

2. Keep a model fixed and collect new observations

A model gives probabilities before the new trials occur. Begin with four equally likely categories, or use the current shares above as a fixed model. Making a model from those shares is an assumption about future trials; it does not prove that the sample represents a larger population.

Model probabilities and observed shares

During a run, the model probabilities stay fixed while the observed proportions change. Expected count equals the number of draws times the model probability. To use new proportions in the simulation, select a model again.

With equal probabilities, draw 100 outcomes. Must each category appear exactly 25 times? Restart and compare a fresh run.

Compare your reasoning

No. The expected counts are 25 each, but observed counts vary. More trials usually bring the proportions closer to the fixed probabilities; they do not guarantee improvement at every step.
