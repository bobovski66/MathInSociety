Enter observations, then look at both center and spread

Load a preset or enter values separated by commas, spaces, or new lines. Each value is one observation. The calculator uses the sample standard deviation, dividing squared deviations by n − 1. With one observation, it can report a mean but cannot calculate a sample SD.

Follow the sample standard deviation

The mean is subtracted from each observation. The final column squares that deviation. Add that column, divide by n − 1, then take the square root.

Change just one value

The first six values stay at 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, and 10. Only the final value changes. Predict which summaries will move before adjusting it.

Changing a value here is an experiment about the summaries. In an actual study, investigate an unusual observation before deciding what to do with it.

Questions to investigate

  1. Compare the two “same mean” presets on the fixed scale. What changes even though both means are 50?
  2. Switch to a fitted scale. How could separately fitted graphs make spreads look more alike than they are?
  3. Load the complaint data, fit the scale, and try different histogram intervals. The observations do not change; what happens to the picture?
  4. Enter three identical values. Explain the range and sample SD. Then try a single observation.
  5. Change the final value in the second experiment. Use the ordered list to explain why the median stays at 8.
